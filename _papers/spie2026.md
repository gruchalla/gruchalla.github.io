SolTrace<sup>TM</sup> is an open-source Monte Carlo ray-tracing software developed by the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) for modeling the optical performance of concentrating solar thermal (CST) collectors as well as other solar applications. The software provides a flexible, technology-agnostic framework capable of simulating diverse collector and receiver configurations to predict spatial and temporal flux distributions which are critical metrics for CST component design, performance evaluation, and system integration. With over 1,650 scholarly citations and nearly 10,000 downloads since 2017, SolTrace has become a cornerstone tool for optical verification and benchmarking within the concentrating solar research community. Despite its extensive adoption, legacy SolTrace faces several practical limitations, including limited documentation, a steep learning curve for new users, and computational constraints that restrict its applicability to large-scale or time-dependent optical analyses. To overcome these challenges, a new generation of SolTrace has been developed. Key architectural enhancements include the implementation of advanced ray-tracing acceleration structures on both the CPU and graphics processing unit (GPU). Complementary usability developments include a completely modernized cross-platform graphical user interface (GUI) built on Qt with high-end interactive 3D visualizations. This paper presents technical architecture, performance benchmarks, validation against established literature round-robin studies, and a comparison of legacy and new SolTrace GUI.
